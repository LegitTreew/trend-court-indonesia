How it works

Every trend goes on trial

We look at 6 kinds of change (the "charges") in 14 cities, from Banda Aceh to Jayapura. A change only counts as real if it beats three common excuses (the "alibis").

  1. 1
    "It's just random ups and downs."

    Weather jumps around every year. We test whether the change is bigger than that normal wobble.

    Fails → No case
  2. 2
    "El Niño did it."

    El Niño years are drier and hotter in most of Indonesia. We subtract El Niño's effect and test again.

    Fails → Acquitted
  3. 3
    "You picked a convenient start year."

    A trend can appear just by choosing where the graph begins. We try every start year; a real trend shows up in most of them.

    Fails → Not proven
  4. ✓
    Beats all three

    The change is real and not explained by El Niño. It doesn't say what caused it, only that it's there.

How the court works

Method and limits

The three alibis
  • Noise: modified Mann-Kendall test that corrects for year-to-year memory (Hamed & Rao, 1998).
  • El Niño: fit y = a + b·year + c·ONI and subtract only c·ONI, then test again.
  • Cherry-picking: repeat the test for every start year. A real trend keeps its direction and stays significant for at least 70% of them.
Data

    Each city's dry season is its own driest 4 months (1981–2010). Rainy-season start uses the BMKG rule: the first 10-day period with at least 50 mm, followed by two more.

    What the court can't tell you
    • NASA POWER values are averages over about 50 km, not rain-gauge readings.
    • El Niño is removed as a straight-line, same-season effect. The Indian Ocean Dipole is not removed yet.
    • "Guilty" means the change is statistically real. It doesn't say what caused it.
