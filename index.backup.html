Is Indonesia changing, or is it just El Niño?

The El Niño Alibi puts every trend in 45 years of NASA data on trial: 6 charges in 14 cities, from Banda Aceh to Jayapura. A trend is convicted only if it survives three alibis.

The three alibis
  • Noise: modified Mann-Kendall test that corrects for year-to-year memory (Hamed & Rao, 1998).
  • El Niño: fit y = a + b·year + c·ONI and subtract only c·ONI, then test again.
  • Cherry-picking: repeat the test for every start year. A real trend keeps its direction and stays significant for at least 70% of them.
Data

    Each city's dry season is its own driest 4 months (1981–2010). Rainy-season start uses the BMKG rule: the first 10-day period with at least 50 mm, followed by two more.

    What the court can't tell you
    • NASA POWER values are averages over about 50 km, not rain-gauge readings.
    • El Niño is removed as a straight-line, same-season effect. The Indian Ocean Dipole is not removed yet.
    • "Guilty" means the change is statistically real. It doesn't say what caused it.
